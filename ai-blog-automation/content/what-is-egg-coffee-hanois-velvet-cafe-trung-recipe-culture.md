# What Is Egg Coffee? Hanoi's Velvet Café Trứng

There is a drink in Hanoi that looks like it belongs in a patisserie case and tastes like a dessert you're embarrassed to admit is a coffee. It is **egg coffee** — *cà phê trứng* — and for decades it was one of Vietnam's best-kept secrets.

Below a frothed, honey-coloured crown of whipped egg yolks and condensed milk sits a small, dark, fiercely strong cup of Vietnamese *robusta* coffee. You don't stir it. You drink it through the foam, letting the sweet whipped clouds melt into the bitter coffee underneath. The warmth, the sweetness, the bite, and the decades of history stack in every sip.

## Where the Story Began

Egg coffee was born in the 1940s, in a Hanoi short on fresh milk. During the French colonial era (and then the harder war years), milk was scarce and expensive. Sweetened condensed milk — imported, shelf-stable, endlessly reliable — became the substitute that Vietnamese coffee is now world-famous for.

But one man went further. In 1946, **Nguyễn Văn Giảng**, working at the Sofitel Legend Metropole as a barman, decided to beat egg yolks into condensed milk and fold the mixture into coffee. The result was a creamy foam that made a bitter, ration-scarce cup taste like velvet.

His recipe lives on at the landmark **Giảng Café** in Hanoi's Old Quarter, the same café that opened in 1946 and still sits beside the family home today. Order egg coffee at Giảng, and you are drinking the original — in a metal cup floating in a bowl of warm water to keep it hot, exactly as it was served breakfast generations.

## Why Egg Coffee Isn't What You'd Expect

If you've never tried it, "egg + coffee" may sound odd, even alarming. The reassurance is in the technique:

- The yolk is **whipped with condensed milk** until it lightens into a thick, ribboned custard.
- Added to **strong, dark Vietnamese coffee** (usually robusta or a robusta-arabica blend), the sweet foam floats on top and slowly melts into the cup.
- The drink is **served hot**, often over ice in the South or as the warm Hanoi original.
- Ice versions (egg coffee *đá*) dilute the foam and turn it into a thicker milkshake — a different, equally beloved animal.

The magic is the yin-and-yang pairing: aggressive bitter coffee + custard-sweet foam. Egg coffee is Vietnam's answer to a tiramisu you drink.

## What to Order, and Where

Not all Vietnamese "egg coffee" is equal. The two poles:

- **Traditional Hanoi egg coffee (nóng)** — foamy crown, hot, dense, almost like a warm mousse on a dark base. You taste the coffee first and the dessert second.
- **Modern/iced egg coffee (đá)** — blended or shaken with ice, thicker, sweeter, closer to a frappé. Instagram-loved, café Vietnam-wide.

Legendary Hanoi spots: **Giảng Café** (the original, 1946), **Cafe Dinh** (a tiny war-era favourite run by the same family), and a growing wave of modern cafés that top the classic with cocoa powder or a dusting of cinnamon.

## Egg Coffee vs. Your Usual Cup

| | Egg Coffee | Black / Piped Vietnamese Coffee |
|---|---|---|
| **Base** | Strong robusta | Robusta or arabica |
| **Sweetness** | Very sweet (condensed milk foam) | None to moderate |
| **Texture** | Custardy, frothed, dessert-like | Lean, dark, direct |
| **When** | Any time; feels like a treat | Morning ritual, post-meal |

Egg coffee is not the "serious" coffee you drink for caffeine alone. It's the coffee you drink when you want the day to feel like a small ceremony.

## Save the Foam, Taste the City

Egg coffee is the rare drink that hides a decade of scarcity inside a dessert. Born from a war-time milk shortage, perfected by a bartender's tinker, and still poured today from the same Hanoi pots — it's Vietnam in a cup: bitter, sweet, resourceful, and quietly world-famous.
